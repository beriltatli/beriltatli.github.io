01 — About

Hi, I’m Beril. Most of what I make starts with a question.

I study electrical-electronics engineering and live in İzmir. Most of what I make starts with a question I can’t stop turning over.

How does a lander reach a point on Mars on the least fuel? Can a small language model make a large one write faster without changing a single word? How do you tell who spoke when, if nobody tells you how many people are in the room?

I answer them by building the thing from the ground up: the attention layers, the CTC loss, the autograd engine, the Kalman filter. Then I measure it and write down what the numbers say, including the targets I miss. A distance means little to me without its error bar.

The other half of my GitHub is drawings you can touch: a Trojan Horse that opens at night, a grand piano that comes apart layer by layer, a château game whose music is generated in code.

Powered descent

A lander has to reach a chosen point on Mars, softly, on as little fuel as possible, and its engine cannot throttle below a minimum. I rebuilt Açıkmeşe and Ploen’s lossless convexification from the 2007 paper, checked it against the theory, timed it like flight software and flew it live in a 3-D simulation.

Fly the simulation
Machine learning · Python1.97×

Speculative decoder

Can a small model help a big one write faster without changing a single word? A speculative decoder written from scratch, with a paged KV cache and exact accept-reject sampling, benchmarked on an Apple M3. On repetitive text it reaches 1.97×, on code about 1.2–1.3×, and on ordinary prose it can be slightly slower. The write-up says so.

Source and benchmark
Machine learning · Python

Transformer, English to Turkish

An encoder-decoder written from primitives: attention, masks, positional encodings, the BPE tokenizer, the learning-rate schedule. The 9.6M-parameter model trained for 34 minutes on an 8 GB MacBook Air, and 22% of its translations of unseen sentences match the human one word for word. It also asks what BLEU really measures when Turkish builds words from stacked suffixes.

Source and report

The oldest trick in the book as an isometric figure. Drag the rope to pull the gift through the gate. Press N for night: a hatch swings down, a rope ladder unrolls and six soldiers climb out to open the city from the inside.

Lumière Şatosu

A third-person exploration game after the châteaux of Île-de-France. A sword has slept in the temple for a hundred years: find the five runes hidden around the château, forge it in the workshop and wake it. Built with three.js and Vite; every sound and every note of music is generated in code with Web Audio.
